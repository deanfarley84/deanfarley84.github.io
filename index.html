The Thesis

Payments is fragmented by design. The fragmentation has a cost, and someone should own recovering it.

The modern payment estate is not one system. It is an accretion of acquirers, gateways, PSPs, fraud vendors, FX desks and reporting layers, each with its own contract, its own data model and its own margin. The silos serve the providers. The merchant pays for them, in blended pricing that hides interchange, in declines nobody reconciles, in FX spread nobody benchmarks, and in fraud tooling that quietly blocks good customers. Nowhere is it sharper than in cross-border commerce, where every new market adds another acquirer, another scheme relationship, another settlement currency and another layer of cost nobody reconciles end to end.

Dean Farley has worked every side of that estate. He has sold the legacy stack at global scale, sold the orchestration layer built to replace it, and now runs an independent practice that answers to no provider at all. He has built and priced cross-border estates across the UK, the EEA, LATAM, North America, APAC and North Africa, and knows exactly where each market's fragmentation hides its cost. That vantage is rare: most people in payments know one seat at the table. Dean has held them all, and it shows in how he diagnoses, how he models, and how he negotiates.

"Merchants were never short of providers. They were short of someone on their side of the table who understands the whole estate and can prove the number."

His output is always the same artefact: a CFO-grade, board-ready ROI. Authorisation uplift, decline and false-decline leakage, true cost of acceptance, FX and DCC economics, fraud and chargeback drag, quantified, defensible, and framed in the language a board signs off. Strategy that does not survive a CFO's scrutiny is not strategy. His does.

Payments revenue recovery

Declines · False declines · Retries · Involuntary churn

Every payment estate leaks. Recoverable declines sit unreconciled, false declines turn good customers away, retry logic fires blind, and subscription businesses bleed involuntary churn that finance books as demand weakness. None of it appears on a standard provider dashboard, because no provider is paid to find it.

Dean's recovery work starts with decline-code forensics: what failed, why, at which issuer, on which rail, and what percentage was genuinely recoverable through smarter routing, retry timing, token refresh or issuer-specific treatment. The output is a recovery value the CFO can audit, which is exactly why VYRE can operate on contingency: the number is real enough to be paid from.

Working depth
  • Decline-code and issuer-response forensics
  • False-decline and good-customer insult-rate analysis
  • Retry strategy: timing, sequencing, rail selection
  • Subscription involuntary-churn recovery
  • Auth-rate benchmarking by market, bin range and vertical
The frameOne percent of auth uplift on a £1bn estate is £10m. Dean models it before anyone asks.

FX and dynamic currency conversion

Cross-border · Settlement currency · Spread · Like-for-like pricing

FX is where payments pricing goes to hide. Cross-border estates carry spread inside settlement, inside DCC, and inside multi-currency pricing structures that are almost never benchmarked like for like. Dean has sold FX and DCC across global acquiring at Worldline and knows both sides of the spread: what the provider earns, and what the merchant could reclaim or share.

Worked properly, DCC is a revenue line for the merchant, not just a provider product, and settlement currency strategy is a margin decision, not an ops default. Dean maps currency flows, benchmarks the true spread against mid-market, and restructures the arrangement so the economics follow the merchant's volume, not the provider's default rate card.

Working depth

Fraud prevention and chargeback economics

3DS2 · Risk rules · AI chargeback management · Insult rate

Fraud tooling has two failure modes and only one of them gets measured. Fraud losses appear in reporting; the good customers blocked by over-tight rules do not. Dean treats fraud as an economic optimisation, not a compliance checkbox: the goal is maximum net approved revenue at an acceptable loss rate, and every rule in the stack either serves that goal or costs money.

At Solidgate he positioned AI-driven chargeback management within the full stack; across a decade of acquiring he has seen how 3DS2 strategy, exemption management and risk-rule design move both fraud and approval simultaneously. The chargeback line itself carries hidden drag: fees, ratios threatening scheme programmes, and ops overhead that rarely makes the business case until Dean puts it there.

Working depth
  • 3DS2 strategy, exemptions and SCA optimisation
  • Risk-rule economics and false-positive cost modelling
  • AI-driven chargeback management and representment
  • Scheme monitoring programmes and ratio management
  • Fraud vendor evaluation in net-revenue terms
The frameThe most expensive fraud tool is the one blocking customers who wanted to pay.
